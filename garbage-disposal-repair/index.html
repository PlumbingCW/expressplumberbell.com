Disposal jammed, humming or leaking right now

A humming sound with no grinding means the motor has power but the flywheel is locked. Something hard, such as a bone, a fruit pit or a utensil, is usually wedged between the shredder ring and the plate. The motor can overheat if it keeps trying, so turn it off.

A leak is a different problem. Water under the sink can come from the sink flange at the top, the dishwasher hose, the drain outlet on the side or a crack in the housing. Only the last one means the unit is finished.

Dry the cabinet, set a bowl under the drip and watch where the new water appears. That tells the plumber more than a guess does.

Safe jam reset with the power off

Most homeowners can try this once. Stop if anything feels wrong and call.

  1. 1

    Cut power

    Turn off the wall switch, then unplug the unit under the sink or switch off its breaker. Never put a hand in the drain, even when it looks off.

  2. 2

    Find the hex socket

    Look under the disposal for a small hex socket in the center of the base. A hex wrench often comes taped to the unit or in the original box.

  3. 3

    Work the wrench

    Insert the wrench and turn it back and forth until the shaft moves freely all the way around.

  4. 4

    Remove the item

    Use tongs or pliers from above to lift out whatever was stuck. Do not use your fingers.

  5. 5

    Press the reset

    Wait a few minutes for the motor to cool, then press the red reset button on the bottom. Restore power and run cold water while testing.

Where disposal leaks come from

The location of the drip points to the part that has failed. Some are cheap fixes, and some mean a new unit.

Dishwasher inletCracked hose or loose clampReplace the hose and clamp

A leak from the base or the housing is a common reason for choosing a new disposal.

Slow drain, bad smell and clogs

If water backs up into the sink and the disposal runs normally, the clog is probably past the unit in the trap or the branch line. Grease, coffee grounds and starchy food scraps such as rice and pasta settle there and harden. Running more water will not clear it.

A smell is usually food film on the shredder ring and splash guard. Ice and citrus peel may freshen it for a day. If gurgling also appears in a neighboring sink or tub, the blockage may be in the main line, which is a job for drain cleaning and sometimes sewer line cleaning.

Dishwasher drain and the air gap

The dishwasher usually drains through the disposal, so a blocked disposal can make the dishwasher leave dirty water at the bottom. The connection point has a knockout plug that must be removed when a new disposal is installed with a dishwasher. A plumber checks that step so the machine drains properly.

The dishwasher drain hose runs up and over before it connects, either through a high loop or an air gap on the countertop. That bend keeps sink water from flowing back into the dishwasher. If the air gap spits water, it is often clogged and needs cleaning.

What affects disposal repair pricing

A jam clearing and a new disposal with a reconnected sink are not the same visit. We quote after the plumber sees the unit.

Repair or new unitA reset or gasket is quick. A replacement adds the disposal itself and more labor.
Size and motor powerA heavier motor handles more food waste and costs more to buy than a basic one.
Existing connectionsThe mounting ring, dishwasher connection and electrical cord may need updating to fit a new unit.
Condition of the sink drainA corroded trap or old slip nuts under the sink are often replaced while the cabinet is open.
Clogs beyond the unitIf the blockage sits in the branch line, cleaning it is a separate task from the disposal.
