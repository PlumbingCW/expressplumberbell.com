What you will find in the guides

Each guide starts with a direct answer, then explains the mechanism behind it. A running toilet, a pinhole in a copper line or a heater that will not stay lit all have causes you can learn to read. Knowing the cause helps you judge whether a repair is a short job or a bigger one.

The guides also cover costs without quoting dollar figures. Instead they list the things that move a price, such as access, parts, fixture type and permits, so you can compare quotes from any plumber with the same questions.

Built around Bell, not a generic template

Housing and water details come from public sources, including Census data and the Golden State Water quality report. Older buildings, a large renter population and very hard water for many addresses all change how plumbing behaves here. Where a fact does not apply to every address, the guide says so.

Topics range from fixing a bathtub faucet and sizing a tankless heater to reading a sewer camera report. The latest posts are listed below, newest first.

Safety comes first in every guide

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility from outside. For active flooding or sewage, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line at (855) 575-2890.
