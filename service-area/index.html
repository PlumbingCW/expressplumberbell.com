Bell, the home base

Bell is a dense city of 33,559 residents on about 2.51 square miles, sitting on the west bank of the Los Angeles River. City Hall is at 6330 Pine Ave, and the Building and Safety Division there handles plumbing permits and inspections. Main streets include Florence Ave, Gage Ave, Pine Ave and Otis Ave, with Bandini Blvd as the industrial corridor.

Bell uses ZIP 90201 for street delivery. ZIP 90202 is a PO box ZIP, so a street address in Bell almost always uses 90201. If a plumbing problem needs a permit, a call to the city building department settles the rules for your address.

Towns with their own page

Bell Gardens sits east of Bell and shares ZIP 90201. Its water comes from two systems, a city-owned section in the north and Golden State Water elsewhere, so your bill tells you which one applies.

Cudahy lies south of Bell and also uses 90201. It is one of the densest cities in the country, and its page covers common rental-housing problems such as shared drains.

Maywood is northwest of Bell and uses ZIP 90270. Lots there often hold more than one dwelling, and the page explains why that matters for sewer laterals and mutual water companies.

Nearby places on the edges of the map

Huntington Park (90255), South Gate (90280), Vernon, Commerce, Downey and the unincorporated community of Florence-Graham all border the towns above. They do not have their own pages, so call the line and give the street address. The dispatcher will confirm whether a plumber can be sent.

Landmarks help you place yourself. Treder Park, Veterans Memorial Park, the Bell Library on Gage Ave and Bell High School are all inside the city, and the Los Angeles River marks the western edge.

Finding out which water company serves your address

The water bill is the quickest answer. The company named on it is the one to call about pressure, quality or a leak on the street side of the meter. This matters because hardness, lead service line records and shutoff rules can differ from one provider to the next.

Only the Golden State Water Bell-Bell Gardens figures are used on this site for hardness, since that is the report that was verified. If you are served by another company, ask it for its annual water quality report instead of assuming the same numbers apply.

Why the area matters for plumbing

Most of this part of Los Angeles County was built up in the middle of the last century. In Bell, about 67.6 percent of housing units predate 1960, and about three quarters are rented. Older drains and shared walls mean leaks and clogs travel between units, so early notice to the landlord is part of the fix.

Water providers differ too. Bell and Bell Gardens are listed in the same Golden State Water system, while Cudahy and Maywood each have several water companies. When a question depends on water quality or pressure, check the bill and call the provider shown on it.
