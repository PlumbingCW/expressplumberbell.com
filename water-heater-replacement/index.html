When the Tank Is Leaking or Flooding Your Floor

Water from the body of the tank usually means the steel shell has failed inside. Take these steps in order while you wait for the plumber.

  • Turn the cold water valve on the pipe above the heater clockwise until it stops.
  • Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or set the gas control to off for a gas unit.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility from outside.
  • Stay out of standing water near outlets, the panel, or any wet appliance.
  • Let the water cool before touching the tank or a drain hose, because it can scald.
  • Photograph the heater, the data plate, and the damage for your insurer or landlord.

Which Kind of Heater Goes Back In

Most homes replace what they have, because the gas line, vent, or electrical circuit is already in place. Changing fuel or type is possible, but it adds work around the heater.

Your plumber can walk through the options at the unit and give a clear price before work starts.

Gas storage tank

This type needs a vent to the outside and a gas shutoff within reach. The existing vent is checked during the swap, since a poor draft is a safety problem.

Electric storage tank

An electric unit needs a circuit sized for its elements, and the plumber confirms the wiring matches the new unit. It has no flue, which helps in tight closets.

Heat pump water heater

The Department of Energy says these run two to three times more efficiently than standard electric resistance heaters. They need at least 1,000 cubic feet of air space and a surrounding temperature of 40 to 90 degrees Fahrenheit all year, so a small cold closet is a poor fit.

Tankless

A tankless unit is a bigger project than a like-for-like swap. See the tankless water heater page for sizing and venting.

Sizing the New Heater to Your Household

Storage heaters carry a first hour rating. That number is the gallons of hot water the unit can supply in one hour of heavy use, and the Energy.gov sizing guide says to pick a model whose rating at least matches your peak hour.

Bell averages 3.49 persons per household by the 2020 Census, so a full house stacking showers, a dishwasher, and laundry in the morning can outrun a small tank. Tell the plumber who lives in the home and when the hot water is used most.

A heater that is too small runs out mid-shower. One that is too large costs more to buy and to keep hot.

What Twelve Grains of Hardness Does to a Tank

The Golden State Water Company 2026 report for the Bell-Bell Gardens system lists hardness from 137 to 280 mg/L, with an average of 210 mg/L, or about 12 grains per gallon. On the USGS scale anything over 180 mg/L is very hard.

Hard water leaves scale on elements and settles as sediment on the tank floor. That is why a heater in a GSWC area can get noisy or slow to heat.

Your bill shows if GSWC or another provider serves you, and addresses on other systems should ask that provider for a hardness figure. If you are on GSWC, ask about a water softener to protect the new unit.

How a Replacement Visit Runs

The visit is built around safety first, then a swap, then testing.

  1. 1

    Make it safe

    The plumber confirms the water, power, and gas are off and checks for gas or water damage around the unit.

  2. 2

    Drain and disconnect

    The old tank is drained to a safe spot and its supply, vent, and gas or electric connections are removed.

  3. 3

    Set the new unit

    The heater goes on a drain pan if the location calls for one, with new connectors and a discharge pipe on the relief valve.

  4. 4

    Restore and test

    Water is turned on and bled of air, then power or gas is restored and the flame or elements are checked.

  5. 5

    Walk you through it

    You are shown the shutoff valve, the temperature setting, and where the data plate and warranty details are.

Bracing and Permits

A water heater can tip in an earthquake, so ask the plumber about seismic strapping on the new tank. It is advice worth following, and it takes little time during the swap.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Bell building department. City Hall is at 6330 Pine Ave and the Building and Safety Division can be reached at (323) 588-6211.
