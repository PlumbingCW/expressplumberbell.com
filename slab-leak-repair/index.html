Signs the Water Is Running Under Your Floor

The classic signs are a warm patch on a floor that is otherwise cool, the faint sound of running water when every tap is off, and a water meter that keeps moving. Damp carpet, lifting laminate, a musty smell or a bill that jumped for no clear reason point the same way.

Any one of these can have another cause. Two or three together make a slab leak the likely suspect.

Do Your Own Meter Check First

EPA WaterSense gives a simple test. Read your water meter, use no water for two hours, then read it again. If the numbers changed, water is going somewhere.

Check for running toilets and dripping taps before you call a slab leak. If those are dry, the leak is likely in a pressurized line. Closing the main valve and watching whether the meter stops tells you if the leak is on the house side.

How a Slab Leak Gets Found

The goal is to pin down the leak within a small area before any concrete is touched.

  1. 1

    Isolate the system

    Fixtures are shut off and the lines are tested one at a time, which narrows the search to hot or cold and to one side of the house.

Why Slab Leaks Happen

Pipes under a slab sit in contact with concrete and soil for decades. Friction from small ground movement, corrosion on the outside of the pipe and pressure on the line can all wear a hole. Hot lines sometimes fail first because heat expands and flexes the metal.

Mineral build-up is another contributor. For addresses on the Golden State Water Bell-Bell Gardens system, the 2026 report lists hardness from 137 to 280 mg/L with a 210 average. That is very hard on the USGS scale, so scale inside older pipe is a reasonable thing to ask about during the visit.

What to Document for Insurance or Your Landlord

Good records speed up any claim and any repair approval.

Meter readings

Write down the date, time and reading before and after the no-use period.

Photos and video

Capture warm spots, damp flooring, stained baseboards and any visible cracks.

Bills

Keep recent water bills. A sudden rise supports the timing of the leak.

Messages

Keep copies of every email, text or letter with the landlord or insurer.

When a Slab Leak Points to Repiping

A single leak on a good line is a repair. Repeat leaks on the same system are different. In that case, repiping the home is worth pricing against paying for repair after repair.

If you are not yet sure the leak is under the slab, start with professional leak detection. When the failure is in an exposed or wall pipe instead, burst pipe repair is the right page.
