Hard Water and Water Heaters in Bell, CA

Golden State Water Company's Bell-Bell Gardens report lists an average hardness of 210 mg/L, which is about 12 grains per gallon. On the USGS scale that is very hard water. Heat makes dissolved minerals drop out as scale, and a water heater is the one appliance in the house that heats water all day.

If your address is served by Golden State Water, expect scale to build up inside the tank and on the heating parts. This guide explains how that happens, what to watch for and what a softener can and cannot do.

What scale does inside a tank heater

Calcium and magnesium dissolve easily in cold water. When the water heats, they come out of solution and settle as a hard crust. In a tank heater, that crust and loose sediment collect at the bottom, where the burner or lower element does its work.

Sediment acts like a blanket between the heat source and the water. The heater has to run harder to reach the same temperature, and you may hear popping or rumbling as steam bubbles push through the layer. Those sounds are a hint that the tank needs attention.

The parts hard water hits first

Not every symptom means the tank is finished. These are the usual trouble spots.

Heating elements in electric tanks

Scale coats the metal and traps heat, so the element runs hotter than it should. It can burn out sooner. See water heater repair for what a plumber checks.

Gas burner area

Sediment at the bottom of a gas tank sits where the flame heats the steel. Noise and slow heating are the common signs.

The anode rod

This rod sits in the tank and corrodes in place of the tank steel. Hard water chemistry can change how fast it wears, so it is worth inspecting during service.

The relief valve and fittings

Mineral buildup can stick a relief valve or leave crust around a fitting. Check for dampness and crust on the top of the heater.

A basic flush, in the order a plumber would do it

Follow your heater's manual first, because details differ by model. This is the general sequence.

  1. 1

    Turn off the power or gas

    For electric, switch off the breaker. For gas, set the control to the pilot or off position as the manual directs.

  2. 2

    Close the cold water supply

    This keeps the tank from refilling while you drain it.

  3. 3

    Connect a hose and open a hot tap

    Run the hose to a safe drain. Opening a hot tap upstairs lets air in so the tank empties.

  4. 4

    Open the drain valve and watch the water

    Gritty or cloudy water points to sediment. If it will not flow, the valve may be clogged with scale, and forcing it can damage it.

  5. 5

    Close, refill and restore power

    Close the drain, open the cold supply, let the tank fill with a hot tap open until water runs steady, then switch the power or gas back on.

Hot water and a clogged drain valve

If the drain valve will not open or leaks after you try it, stop and call a plumber. Forcing a scaled valve is a common way to create a leak where there was none. The water inside the tank is also hot enough to cause burns, so keep hoses and people clear.

What a softener can and cannot do

A water softener removes the calcium and magnesium that cause scale. It will not clean scale that is already in the tank, and it does not filter other things from the water. The two jobs are different, as our page on water softener and filtration explains.

Softeners also need salt, space and maintenance, and renters usually cannot add one without the landlord's approval. For many households, a regular flush and a check of the anode rod is the more realistic plan.

Deciding between flush, repair and replacement

A heater that makes noise but still heats well often needs a flush and inspection. One that leaks from the tank body, as opposed to a fitting or valve, is usually past repair. Our water heater replacement page covers that decision. Energy.gov water heating guidance from the Department of Energy is a neutral reference to read before you spend on treatment or a new heater.

Check the manufacturer date on the data plate and the warranty paperwork before spending money. Do not rely on an age guess, because hard water use history varies from home to home. Households near Bell Gardens should confirm which of the city's two water systems feeds their street, since the GSWC hardness figure covers only one part.
