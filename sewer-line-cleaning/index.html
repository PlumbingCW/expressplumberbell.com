Sewage backup tonight: what to do before the plumber arrives

These four moves limit the mess and make the visit go smoother.

1

Stop all water

No toilets, showers, sinks, washers or dishwashers. Every gallon you send down has nowhere to go.

2

Protect the room

Lift rugs and belongings off the floor, and put towels at the low edges of the affected area.

3

Keep people clear

Children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system should stay out of standing sewage. Wash hands well after contact.

4

Document it

Take photos and note the time. You may need them for a landlord, a property manager or an insurer.

Reading the backup: which drain tells you where the clog sits

Water takes the easiest path out. When the main line is blocked, wastewater rises at the lowest opening in the building, which is often a bathtub, shower or floor drain on the ground level. Fixtures higher up may only gurgle or drain slowly.

That pattern is why a main line problem is easy to mistake for a sink problem. If a plunger on one fixture changes nothing and a second drain acts up, the blockage is past the branch lines. A fixture-level cable from regular drain cleaning cannot reach it, and a larger machine is needed.

Cleanouts and how the plumber gets in

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the sewer line. It is often outside near the house, though in some buildings it sits inside or at a roof vent. If you can find yours, tell the plumber, and do not open it yourself because wastewater may rise out of the opening.

With no cleanout, the plumber may need to pull a toilet or use another access point. That adds time, which is one of the factors on the cost list below.

Equipment used on a main line

The plumber chooses between three approaches. A heavy cable machine with a cutting head breaks through roots and thick buildup. A hydro jet washes grease and silt from the pipe wall, and a drain camera shows the cause.

Often the work combines methods. A cable restores flow quickly, a jet cleans the pipe, and a camera look afterward confirms what is left. If the camera shows damage, the plumber explains the repair options on the sewer line repair page.

Storm drains and yard drains are a separate system

Sewer lines carry wastewater from the building. Storm drains and downspout lines carry rain, and they are cleaned for a different reason, usually silt and leaves. Yard drains that clog can flood a patio or driveway.

If a drain outside your property seems to be the problem, ask Bell Public Works whether it is a city or private line. Do not assume. A camera can trace a private line, and the plumber can tell you what the pipe is connected to.

Tell the right people

A backup affects more than the plumber and you.

Landlord or property manager

Send a written note with photos and the time. In a multi-unit building, a main line blockage can affect neighbors too.

Neighbors in the same building

If you share a line, a quick word helps them stop water use before wastewater rises in their unit.

Your insurer

Ask whether the policy covers sewage backup damage. Not every policy does.

The city

If you think the blockage is in a city line, ask Bell Public Works where your responsibility ends.

Shared lines in Bell's rental housing
