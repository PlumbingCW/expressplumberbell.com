The call we hear most, a shared drain that backs up

Census figures show Cudahy has about 3.80 people per household and only 14.2 percent of homes owner occupied. State housing data lists 27.3 percent of the city's 5,780 units in buildings of five or more units. Many households therefore share drain lines, and one clogged branch can send water into the lowest unit.

The warning signs are a toilet that gurgles when a washer drains, slow tubs on a ground floor, or sewage smell near a floor drain. Stop running water in the building. That keeps the backup from growing while the cause is found.

The fix may be cabling or jetting a shared line, and the plumber can run a camera to see what is blocking it. See our page on drain cleaning for the methods, and tell the property manager at once.

Why this matters in a dense city

Cudahy covers about 1.18 square miles, and its history includes narrow lots, sometimes 50 to 100 feet wide, laid out after Michael Cudahy bought Rancho San Antonio land in 1908. With homes this close together, a shared line can carry one household's problem to the next. Calling early gives the plumber a chance to stop it before it spreads.

Leaks you cannot see

In tight buildings a leak hides inside walls, under floors and above ceilings. The EPA WaterSense program says about 9 percent of homes have leaks wasting 50 gallons or more per day, and the average household leaks more than 9,300 gallons a year. The simple meter test is to read the meter, use no water for two hours, then read it again.

If the number moves, water is going somewhere. A leak detection visit uses listening and pressure checks to find the spot before anyone cuts into a wall. For a multi-unit building, ask the manager to confirm which meter serves which unit.

Before we arrive at your Cudahy address

These steps protect your home and your neighbors while the plumber is on the way.

1

Stop the water

Close the shutoff at the fixture. If a pipe is bursting, close the main or ask the manager to close the building shutoff.

2

Warn the neighbors

In a shared building, knock on the units below and beside you. Water travels through floors.

3

Save the evidence

Photograph wet walls, ceilings and personal items for your renter's insurance and your landlord.

4

Clear the way

Move cars, boxes and furniture so the plumber can reach the cleanout, valve or fixture.
