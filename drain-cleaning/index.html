What to do while you wait for the plumber

A few quiet minutes of prep keep a bad clog from turning into a wet floor. None of these steps need tools.

  • Stop using the blocked fixture and ask everyone in the home to hold off on showers and laundry
  • Remove standing water from a sink with a cup and bucket instead of letting it sit
  • Clear the cabinet under the sink so the plumber can reach the trap and wall pipe
  • Put towels around a toilet or floor drain that is close to overflowing
  • Skip chemical drain openers, since the liquid can sit in the pipe and splash on the plumber
  • Note which other fixtures gurgle or slow down, because that pattern points to the line the clog sits in

Kitchen sink clogs and what grease does inside the pipe

Kitchen drains fail differently from bathroom drains. Cooking fat leaves the sink as a warm liquid, then cools and sticks to the pipe wall. Food particles and soap cling to that sticky coat, and the opening shrinks a little more with every sink of dishes.

The result is a drain that first runs slowly, then gurgles, then stops. A plunger can push a soft blockage a short way, but it rarely removes the coating that caused it. That is why a sink can clear for a day and clog again by the weekend.

A cable machine cuts through the buildup and pulls out most of it. If the pipe wall is heavily coated, the plumber may suggest hydro jetting so water pressure scrapes the whole length clean.

Snaking, jetting or a camera: how the plumber chooses

A drain snake, also called a cable or auger, is a coiled steel line that spins through the pipe. It is the first tool for most fixture clogs because it is quick and works in tight spaces. Its limit is that it often bores a hole through a blockage and leaves the pipe wall coated.

Hydro jetting sends high-pressure water through a hose and washes the pipe interior. It suits grease, scale and silt, and it is less suited to damaged pipe. A drain camera is the third option, and it answers a different question, which is what is actually in the pipe.

When a camera comes first

If a drain has been snaked and has clogged again, a camera look shows whether roots, a bellied section or a broken joint is the real cause. A sewer camera inspection also helps before any jetting on older lines, which keeps pressure away from weak pipe.

What not to pour or flush

Most repeat clogs trace back to a few habits that are easy to change.

Cooking fat and oil

Wipe pans with a paper towel and bin it. Liquid fat cools inside the pipe and hardens.

Coffee grounds and rice

Grounds pack into a dense paste and rice swells with water. Neither belongs in a kitchen drain.

Wipes marked flushable

They hold together far longer than toilet paper and catch on rough pipe walls. Put them in the trash.

Hair in the shower

A cheap strainer over the shower drain catches most of it. Empty it every few days.

Harsh chemical openers

They can sit in a fully blocked pipe, irritate skin and make a later repair more hazardous.

When the whole house backs up, it is not a sink problem

If a shower gurgles when the toilet flushes, or water shows up in a floor drain after the washer runs, the blockage is probably in the main drain line. A fixture-level cable will not reach it. The plumber needs a larger machine and access through a cleanout or a roof vent.

That job is covered on the sewer line cleaning page. Treat it as urgent, because waste can come up through the lowest drain in the building. Stop using water and call the 24/7 line.

Shower and tub clogs, and the small leak you might miss

Tub drains collect hair around the overflow and strainer, and the pipe wall behind it grows a layer of soap residue. Pull the strainer first, since a hair mat there is the cause more often than a deep clog.

Slow drains can also hide a leak. If the ceiling below a bathroom is stained, or the floor around the tub feels soft, a drain line may be leaking and not only clogged. A leak behind a wall needs a different tool, and our leak detection service covers it.
