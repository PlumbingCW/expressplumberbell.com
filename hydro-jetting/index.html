The snake cleared it, and then it clogged again

This is the most common reason people call about jetting. A cable bores a hole through a blockage and the water drains, so the job looks finished. The sludge on the pipe wall stays behind, and a few weeks later the hole closes up.

Three causes sit behind most repeat clogs. Grease hardens into a thick lining. Tree and shrub roots enter at joints and grow into a mat. Mineral scale and silt narrow the pipe from the sides.

If any of these sound familiar, the pipe needs to be washed, not just poked. That is what jetting does.

Signs the line needs more than a snake

Slow drains in more than one room, a gurgle from a tub when the sink empties, and a sewer smell near a floor drain all point to buildup along the line. Water standing in a floor drain after laundry is another sign. If sewage is coming up, treat it as an emergency and call right away.

Before help arrives, stay clear and stop the flow

If a line is backing up, a few safe steps keep you away from sewage and high-pressure equipment.

  • Stay clear of the cleanout cap, because a blocked line can be under pressure
  • Do not rent or run pressure equipment yourself, since a jet hose can injure and damage pipe
  • Stop running water, flushing and laundry until the line is cleared
  • Skip chemical drain openers, because they can splash back and are unsafe around a jet
  • Keep children and pets away from wet floors and floor drains
  • Move rugs and boxes off the floor and take dated photos for a landlord or insurer

What hydro jetting does inside the pipe

A hydro jet pushes a hose through the line and fires pressurized water from a nozzle. The nozzle has jets facing backward, which pull the hose forward, and jets that spray the pipe wall. The water cuts through grease, loosens scale and carries debris out toward the cleanout or main.

Compared with a cable, the water reaches the whole circumference of the pipe instead of one channel down the middle. Roots are cut back as well, though they can grow again if the joint stays open. A camera look afterward shows how clean the pipe is.

When jetting is the wrong choice

High-pressure water is not for every pipe. A cracked, offset or collapsed section can be damaged further by the force, and an old clay or cast iron line may have weak spots you cannot see from the surface. In those cases the plumber runs a sewer camera inspection first and may recommend repair instead.

If a camera shows a break, jetting only moves the problem. A repair is the honest answer, and the sewer line repair page covers those options.

If sewer odor or backup shows up tonight

Use these steps while you wait for the plumber. They keep the problem smaller.

  • Stop running water, including laundry, dishwasher and long showers
  • Keep children and pets away from any standing water or wet floor
  • Do not flush the toilets again if water is rising in the tub or floor drain
  • Open a window if there is a strong sewer smell, and do not light matches or candles near gas appliances
  • If you smell natural gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility from outside
  • Take photos of any water damage for your landlord or insurer

Why older Bell lines are a jetting candidate

According to Census ACS data, about 67.6% of Bell's housing units were built before 1960, with roughly 26% from 1939 or earlier. Drain lines in homes of that age have had many decades of grease, roots and scale. That makes repeat clogs plausible and also makes a camera look before jetting sensible.

If you rent, which is true for about 75% of Bell households in the same survey, send a note to your landlord about repeat clogs. A written record of the dates helps both of you decide on a lasting fix.

Hydro jetting and the rest of your drain care

Jetting fits best after a smaller method has failed or when you know buildup is the cause. For a single stopped sink, drain cleaning with a cable is usually enough. For a backup across the house, sewer line cleaning covers the larger main-line equipment.

Keeping grease out of the kitchen drain and wipes out of the toilet makes any cleaning last longer. Roots are the exception, since they return unless the pipe is repaired or treated.
