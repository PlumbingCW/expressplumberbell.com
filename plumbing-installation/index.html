Installations We Take On

These are the most common planned jobs. Each begins with a short conversation about what you have and what you want.

Bathroom plumbing

New sinks, toilets, showers, tubs and the supply and drain lines behind them.

Kitchen and laundry

Sink, dishwasher and disposal hookups, washer boxes and drains.

Outdoor faucets

Hose bibs and yard lines, set up with shutoffs so they can be isolated.

Water heater hookups

Connections, valves and discharge piping for a new heater, tied to the install page.

Gas lines

Appliance connections and line work by a licensed plumber, with a safety check after.

Backflow devices

Installation and testing of protection that keeps water from reversing into the supply.

If You Smell Gas, Stop Reading

Leave the building at once. Do not flip switches, use a lighter or start a car in an attached garage. Call 911 and the gas utility from outside.

Once the gas company has declared the area safe, a licensed plumber can look at the appliance connection or line. Gas work is never a do-it-yourself job.

How a Planned Install Works

Backflow Prevention and Testing

A backflow device keeps water from moving backward into the supply. Irrigation systems, fire lines and some commercial equipment commonly need one, and the device is tested on a schedule set by the local water provider.

Requirements vary by property, so ask your water company or the Bell building department whether yours is required and how often it is tested. A licensed plumber can install the device and perform the test when one is due.

Older Buildings and What They Mean for New Work

Census figures show about 67.6% of Bell housing units were built before 1960. In a house or small building from that era, the plumber should check what is already in the wall before adding to it, because new fixtures tied into very worn lines can inherit the problems.

If that review shows tired pipe, ask about repiping options while the area is open. For the permit step, the Building and Safety Division at City Hall, 6330 Pine Ave, enforces the city plumbing code.

What Decides the Price of an Installation

Two installs of the same fixture can price very differently. These are the factors.

Fixture and materialsHigher-end fixtures and special valves cost more to buy and can take longer to set.
Access and layoutNew lines in open framing are easier than in finished walls or under a slab.
Distance to existing linesThe farther a fixture sits from supply and drain, the more pipe and labor.
Permit and testingInspections and device tests are separate steps.
Finish workTile, drywall, paint and cabinetry are separate trades.

Planned installations are typically not covered by homeowner insurance. Coverage applies to sudden accidental damage, not upgrades or worn parts. Read your policy or ask the insurer. Renters should check with the landlord first, since changes to the plumbing usually need the owner's approval.

New Construction and Additions

For a new unit, an accessory dwelling or an addition, the plumbing plan comes before framing is closed. The plumber needs the fixture list, the locations and the connection points to the existing house.

Bring plans if you have them. If the project also involves water heating, see our notes on water heater installation, and if drains in the older part of the home are slow, drain cleaning is worth doing before you tie in.
