What the Atlantic Avenue Corridor is

The City of Bell uses the name Atlantic Avenue Corridor for the stretch covered by its New Bell District Specific Plan. The city's planning page says the plan is meant to guide commercial and mixed-use developments, meaning buildings that combine shops with housing.

This is a commercial street, not a residential neighborhood in the usual sense. Many buildings here hold a business on the ground floor, and some may have apartments above. That mix matters for plumbing because a shop and a home can share a roof, a drain stack or a water line.

What is documented and what is not

Documented by the City of Bell: the corridor runs along Atlantic Avenue between Randolph Street and E. Florence Avenue, and the city has published a Final Initial Study and Mitigated Negative Declaration dated 10-24-25 and a draft Specific Plan dated 11-20-25. The city page does not state whether the plan has been adopted.

Not documented: which buildings on the street have older pipes, which are mixed-use today, or which water company serves each address. Do not assume any of this from the street name. A plumber has to look at the building.

Services that fit this kind of building

Most calls from commercial streets start with water that is not draining. Drain cleaning clears a blockage, and hydro jetting is one option when a line keeps clogging with grease or buildup. If a camera look is needed, sewer line repair covers what happens when a main line is damaged.

Hidden water loss is the other common call. A leak detection visit helps find a leak inside a wall or slab without opening more of the building than needed. For hot water, see water heater replacement when a tank has failed.

Permits, water service and who to ask

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code. Plumbing permits in Bell are handled by the City of Bell Building and Safety division at City Hall, 6330 Pine Avenue. Ask the city whether your repair needs a permit before work starts, especially for water heater replacement or any change to drain lines.

Water service in Bell comes from more than one provider, including Golden State Water Company and Tract 180 Mutual Water Company. We have not confirmed which one serves each address on Atlantic Avenue, so check your bill.

Before you call about a commercial building

A few details make the first phone call faster.

1

Street and cross street

Give the nearest cross street so the building is easy to identify.

2

Building use

Say whether it is a shop, restaurant, office or mixed-use building with apartments.

3

What you see

Describe standing water, smells, slow drains or stains, and which fixtures are affected.

4

Who can approve work

Tenants often need the landlord or manager to approve repairs. Have that contact ready.
