Supply line burst or valve stuck open

Faucets themselves rarely cause floods. The flexible supply hoses under the sink and the small stop valves that feed them do. A braided hose can split at the fitting, and an old stop valve can seize halfway so it neither opens nor closes.

If water is spraying, shut the main for the whole home first, then sort out the fixture. The main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the property, often near the meter or the front wall. Learn where yours is before you need it.

Once the flow is stopped, open a faucet at the lowest point in the home to drain the pipes. Move cabinet contents and photograph the damage before drying anything out.

Before the plumber gets there

A few minutes of work now keeps a small failure from becoming a large one.

  • Close the stop valve under the sink, or the main shutoff if it will not turn
  • Turn the handle of the damaged faucet to open so pressure bleeds off
  • Empty the cabinet under the sink and dry the floor of it
  • Switch off power to nearby outlets if water has reached them and you can do so safely
  • Photograph the fixture, the damaged area and the wet surfaces
  • Do not force a stuck valve with a pipe wrench, because the stem can snap

How hard water wears out faucets

Minerals that settle out of water leave scale inside cartridges and on aerators. The Golden State Water report for the Bell-Bell Gardens system lists hardness from 137 to 280 mg/L, with an average of 210. On the USGS scale, anything over 180 is very hard.

That is why a faucet at an address on that system may feel stiff, sputter or drip sooner than one in a soft water area. Your water bill shows whether Golden State Water or another provider serves you.

Clearing a clogged aerator is a safe DIY job. Unscrew it by hand, soak it in vinegar and rinse. If the handle itself is gritty or still leaks, the cartridge is likely scarred and needs replacing.

Shower and tub valves behind the wall

A shower that drips from the spout or changes temperature on its own usually has a worn cartridge behind the handle. Many cartridges can be swapped without opening the wall. The plumber removes the handle and trim, pulls the cartridge and fits a matching one.

If the valve body is cracked or corroded, access is needed from behind the wall or through a tile cut. That is a larger job, and the plumber explains it before starting. A wet wall or ceiling below may also need leak detection to confirm where the water travels.

Tenants, landlords and property managers

About 75 percent of Bell homes are rented, so many faucet calls come from tenants. Report a failed valve in writing right away and attach photos. Ask the owner who will authorize the repair so the visit is not delayed.
