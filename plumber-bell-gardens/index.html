Built in the 1940s and 1950s, plumbed for decades

Census survey figures from 2007 to 2009 show that about 46 percent of Bell Gardens homes were built before 1960. Roughly 6.7 percent date from 1939 or earlier, 17.5 percent from the 1940s and 21.5 percent from the 1950s. Another 16.4 percent came in the 1960s.

Over several decades, a home collects repairs and partial replacements. That makes a plumber's first step a look at what is actually in place, not an assumption.

If your home is in that older group, a small leak or slow drain deserves attention earlier than it would in newer construction. Ask the plumber what condition the visible pipes are in before you decide on any bigger work.

Two water systems in one small city

Bell Gardens covers about 2.46 square miles but runs on two systems. The city owns the northern third, and Liberty Utilities handles operations at 800-727-5987. Golden State Water serves the other two thirds at 800-999-4033.

Hardness numbers apply only to the Golden State part. That supply, shared with Bell, ranged from 137 to 280 mg/L in the 2026 report covering 2025, averaging 210 mg/L or 12 grains per gallon. The USGS calls anything over 180 very hard, so heaters and faucets in that area collect scale faster.

If your bill comes from the city system, ask Liberty Utilities for hardness information. The city also keeps its lead service line inventory at the Public Works yard on Garfield Ave, so ask there if you want to know about your line.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak

Before we arrive, take these steps. They limit damage and give the plumber a clear picture.

1

Shut off the water

Close the valve nearest the leak. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, close the main for the home.

2

Turn off the water heater

Switch off power to an electric heater or set a gas heater to pilot or off. This protects the unit if the tank drains.

3

Protect belongings

Move furniture and electronics out of the wet area and lay towels where water is spreading.

4

Document the damage

Photograph the source and wet surfaces. Send them to your landlord or insurer.

Gage Ave, Garfield Ave and the commercial side

Bell Gardens is home to Casa de Rancho San Antonio at 7000 Gage Ave, a State Historical Monument, as well as the Bicycle Hotel & Casino. The city also has 8 parks and one golf course. Parks, a golf course and commercial properties use far more water than a house, so a hidden leak on a service line can run a long time before a bill shows it.

If you manage a business, a shop or a multi-tenant building, our commercial plumbing service covers after-hours leaks and drain overflows. Property owners with irrigation should ask about a meter test, which the EPA describes as reading the meter, using no water for two hours and reading it again.

Permits and checking a plumber

Replacing a water heater or changing piping may need a permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Bell Gardens building department before work starts. The California Plumbing Code is adopted statewide and based on the UPC, which is the standard those local permit rules build on.

You can verify any plumber's license at cslb.ca.gov, where the License Check tool shows whether it is active and lists a plumbing classification. We also cover Cudahy and Maywood, and the same dispatch handles those calls.
